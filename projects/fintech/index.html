Enterprise Digital Banking Platform

Adapting and optimizing banking flows on a modular enterprise platform

Product Design Enterprise UX Design System Banking

Backbase is a platform that banks use as the foundation for their digital products. My work was not building from scratch, it was adapting existing flows for two clients with very different users and business rules, without breaking the shared system both products relied on.

B2C (Peru)
A financial inclusion product for a state-owned bank, built as a mobile app. Many users were opening a bank account for the first time, with only a national ID and a smartphone. Clarity and accessibility were the main focus.

B2B (United States)
A commercial banking and treasury web platform for business clients. Flows covered wire transfers, ACH, FX, and payment scheduling, with strict business and regulatory rules throughout.

How do you adapt the same platform for a first-time banking user in Peru and a treasury manager in the US, without fragmenting the product?

"An innovative project focused on the bankarization and financial inclusion of the Peruvian population, designed to significantly reduce the friction related to traditional bank accounts."

Banco de la Nación, via Backbase

What I worked on

I owned UX strategy and interaction design for both products. Before touching any screen, I mapped each market throug service blueprints and journey maps to understand the business rules and where the real friction was. Then I worked with PMs, BAs, engineering leads, and banking stakeholders to turn those cosntraints into something usable.

A big part of the work was deciding what to reuse, what to extend, and what to push back on. Every time something new was requested, the first question was whether it was actually a new problem or just a familiar one in a different context.

Example decision

For the B2B permissions model, the initial ask was to build custom components. After mapping the actual use cases, the existing components could handle everything if we changed the information hierarchy instead. That kept the system intact and saved weeks of implementation work.

Key challenges

The Peru product needed to work for people with no banking experience. The onboarding had regulatory steps that could not be removed, so the work was about sequencing them in a way that built context before asking for information, instead of making users feel like they were just filling out a form.

The US product had a different problem: multiple users with different roles accessing the same accounts, with complex permission logic that had to stay invisible to each person unless it was directly relevant to them.

Everything was documented as it was built, so the teams that continued the work after launchh could understand not just what was built, but why.

Reflection

Working on an enterprise platform is mostly about judgment, not creativity. knowing when to reuse, when to extend, and when to stay no because something would create problems later is what actually moves the work forward. That only comes from spending real time in the system, not just in the screens.
